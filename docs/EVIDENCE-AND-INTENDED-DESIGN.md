# Evidence and intended design

An episode must represent the experience the capability should deliver, even
when current platform mechanics cannot yet deliver it. Constraints become
engineering work; they do not reduce the intended experience.

Every Season 1 lesson makes three things inspectable:

1. **Current evidence.** What exact capsule bytes and observed receipts establish
   today. A stored expression is decision semantics; a fixture assertion is an
   expectation; a status label is not evidence of the effect its name suggests.
2. **Intended experience.** The complete human outcome and the architecture needed
   to create it. Aspirational behavior is a `TARGET_DESIGN` claim, visibly labeled
   at the point of depiction. Its simulation does not establish implementation.
3. **Missing closure.** The mechanic, provider, enforcement boundary, or evidence
   needed to connect current behavior to the target. Each gap has acceptance
   criteria suitable for an engineering handoff.

Current source evidence uses solid cyan lines with attached source pointers.
Target design uses violet outlines and an explicit target label. Gaps use amber
interrupted boundaries. A closure animation retains the target label throughout;
an animated checkmark never upgrades a requirement into a passed test.

Every article, short, training exercise, diagram, and demo preserves this same
distinction. Narrative compression cannot merge `ALLOW` from the current capsule
with `PERMIT` from the target interlock. Evidence remains evidence of its exact
scope, not of an adjacent cinematic portrayal.

Episode 1 preserves the desired payoff: publication stays pending; an authorized
inspection completes; the engineer gets evidence and retains publication control.
Its interactive reference model classifies seven authored cases. The model
performs no real tool dispatch. Six closure gaps are recorded in
`evaluations/episode-01-platform-gap.json`, with exact source references.

The season curriculum binds this format in `declarations/season-1.json`.
Episode contracts bind the current source, intended design, and gap report.
Platform improvements proceed through the platform's governed lifecycle; content
creation does not itself admit or publish a capability change.
