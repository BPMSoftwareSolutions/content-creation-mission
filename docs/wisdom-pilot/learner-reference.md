# Reasoning about a consequential choice

**WP-01 v1 · Give this page only after all initial and transfer responses are collected.** These are proposed learning lenses, not a certification of wisdom or moral correctness.

Start with the situation. What does a person need? What action is proposed, who may decide, who experiences the result, and what would show that the result happened? Consider a small step, postponement, a different approach, or no action. Being allowed to act does not establish that acting is right.

Ask whether a model helps before asking whether to automate. A narrative may preserve a person's perspective better than a diagram. A limited table can clarify claims while leaving dissent and unknowns in prose. Neither a diagram nor a persuasive explanation grants decision rights.

| Plain question | Proposed lens | Technical connection introduced after practice |
|---|---|---|
| A step finished; did the person get the result they needed? | Consequences | Mechanic completion is distinct from the scenario outcome. Check intended effects, side effects, affected people, and what cannot be recovered. |
| What does this information actually support? | Humility | Separate observation, inference, assumption, and unknowns. An attributable record still needs interpretation within its scope. Confidence supplies neither missing evidence nor permission. |
| What does the strongest objection establish, and how far does it reach? | Adversarial survivability | Compare a counterexample and a simpler alternative. Revise or retain a judgment for stated reasons. A failed proposal does not show that every alternative fails. |

The wider proposal includes durability, coherence, reciprocity, and evidence sensitivity. These three lessons do not establish mastery of those seven tests, and no combination certifies justice. The lenses can conflict; state whose interests and values differ, compare feasible alternatives, preserve dissent, and identify who is responsible for the unresolved decision.

When using an automated provider, the same questions remain. A success message supports only what it actually reports. Evidence should connect the specific action, circumstances, and claimed result. A human decision can also be mistaken. Reversing a setting does not necessarily recover lost time or undo an experience.

Practice a compact explanation: “I propose ___ because ___. This information supports ___ but does not establish ___. The people affected are ___. The decision belongs to ___ within ___. An alternative is ___. I would reconsider if ___.” Use prose instead of filling every blank when the structure distorts the case.
