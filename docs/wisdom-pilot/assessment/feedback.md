# Feedback exemplars and provisional anchors

**WP-01 v1 · Assessor only until responses are frozen.** Exemplars below are authored examples, not collected learner data. Read quoted feedback to learners only after collecting all initial and transfer responses. Keep branded terms and category labels out of that feedback.

## What the cards elicit

Five dimensions follow strategy §8. Use **supported**, **partly supported**, **unsupported**, or **not elicited** as provisional descriptions of reasoning, never a learner grade. An absent answer to a question actually asked is unsupported or partly supported with a reason; an unasked question is not elicited. Cite case facts and learner words. Confidence, word count, preferred policy, and agreement with the instructor supply no credit.

| Dimension | Where it is intentionally elicited | Case-specific focus |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario and purpose | A, B, C cards | Understand the stated task/claim; do not invent facts that solve it. |
| Decision rights and boundaries | Worked examples only | Assessment cards do not ask who can authorize a change: mark not elicited by default. Do not transfer practice performance into an assessment judgment. |
| Evidence and uncertainty | A and B cards | A: scope of completion evidence and further checks. B: distinction between the supplied record and inferred claim. C's reasoning critique is assessed below, not double-counted as evidence assessment. |
| Consequences and reciprocity | A cards | Distinguish the person's usable result and affected experience from a completed step. B and C prompts generally do not elicit this dimension; mark not elicited. |
| Reconsideration under challenge | C paired cards | Engage the specific argument; justify revision or adequate prior judgment. Judge broad overreach separately when supplied. A/B independent cards have no staged objection. |

The later C-L1 overlaps consequences, but its requested response still targets critique and reconsideration; note spontaneous consequence reasoning without adding a second score. Any departure from this elicitation map needs a cited prompt-specific reason and must be visible in calibration disagreements.

## A exemplars — completion and the needed result

| Authored response | Descriptive reasoning and feedback |
|---|---|
| “Invitations were sent, so everyone has what they need.” | Unsupported result claim: stated access failures contradict it. Feedback: “Which part of the story shows that people can use the instructions?” |
| “Some people cannot open the instructions. Send them again.” | Recognizes the affected group, but resending alone may not fix access; partly supported next step. Feedback: “What would tell you whether sending again actually helped?” |
| “Sending happened, but usable instructions are missing for some invitees. Ask which access attempt failed and check a usable version with those readers. Do not claim everyone can use it from one person's success.” | Supported distinction and bounded check. Feedback: “Your conclusion separates sending from being able to use the instructions and limits what a sample can establish.” |

For A-T1, “logged” does not establish that a book is located and available to the next reader. Checking location and offering a feasible alternative can be justified. Do not require blaming the returns worker, claiming theft, or promising recovery. A missing book undermines availability without proving every return record false. Use the same reasoning distinctions for other A cards, against their specific facts.

## B exemplars — what the information supports

| Authored response | Descriptive reasoning and feedback |
|---|---|
| “The room is empty now because the photo shows it.” | Unsupported extension to the present. Feedback: “What connects the time of that photograph to the room now?” |
| “We can know nothing; photos can be fake.” | Excessive rejection: a possibility of error is not proof of fabrication. Feedback: “What narrower statement could you make about the image while keeping its limits visible?” |
| “It depicts an empty room at its stated date, assuming the date and image are reliable. It does not establish present occupancy. I would need a current check and enough context to know it is the same room.” | Supported bounded interpretation and relevant uncertainty. Feedback: “You explain what the image supports and which extra claim would need another check.” |

For B-T1, distinguish the excerpt's words from authorship, context, time, and current endorsement. A direct dated confirmation may help, but no required method is unique. “The person must disagree now” is as unsupported as “still agrees.” Reviewers should accept concise explanations that identify the actual unsupported extension without demanding every conceivable uncertainty.

## C exemplars — challenge without reflexive surrender

| Authored response pair | Descriptive reasoning and feedback |
|---|---|
| Initial: “It works.” After challenge: “It still works because approval is responsible.” | Does not address the regress; unsupported response to challenge. Feedback: “Under the rule, what permits the first approval to happen?” |
| Initial: “It works.” After: “The critic wins; all approvals are pointless.” | Changes answer without respecting the argument's scope. Feedback: “Does the objection concern every approval arrangement, or this rule's requirement for another prior approval?” |
| Initial: “It cannot start without a stopping point.” After: “That is the problem I identified. A starting authorization or exempt class could be proposed, but that changes the stated rule.” | Supported retention of an already adequate position. Feedback: “You connect the objection to the rule and distinguish a proposed repair from what the original rule allowed.” |
| Initial: “It works.” After: “I missed that approvals also need approval. The rule cannot start as written. The club would need to decide a bounded alternative; this does not settle which decisions should require approval.” | Supported correction, with limits retained. Feedback: “You identify why your conclusion changed and leave the broader policy question open.” |

For C-T1, exclusive immediate attention to two simultaneous requests cannot be provided by one editor as promised. Queuing, priorities, or revising the promise can address the conflict; do not silently add an editor or redefine “exclusive.” The argument does not establish that all scheduling fails. Full support under reconsideration addresses both objections. Engaging one while ignoring the other is partly supported; changing an answer alone supplies no evidence of understanding.

Alternative conclusions remain acceptable when explicitly conditional and consistent with the supplied facts. If a learner proposes a different interpretation of “separate approval” or “immediately,” record it; distinguish an ambiguity in the prompt from an error. Keep unresolved interpretation disagreements in the calibration report.
