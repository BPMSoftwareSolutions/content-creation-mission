# Substitute and later-case bank — assessor only

**WP-01 v1.** Strip metadata and issue only the selected card. These are fictional cases, not operational instructions. Check exposure to the underlying problem as well as exact text. Versions below are all 1. No statistical equivalence or difficulty calibration is claimed. Substitute use must be reported separately; never pool it silently with primary results.

For C cards, save the initial answer before revealing the challenge paragraph. For all cards, use the [common dimension rules](feedback.md); unasked dimensions are not elicited. Brief anchors below are withheld from learners.

## A-D2 — substitute diagnostic

**Card:** A project helper organizes a shared folder by moving old drafts into an archive. The move finishes. Several teammates say links in their current notes no longer open. What has been completed, what do teammates still need, and what should be checked before calling this helpful?

**Withheld anchor:** distinguish file organization from usable access; inspect which links/people were affected. Undoing the move might restore some links but does not erase lost time. This introduces a harmful side effect of a successful step, rather than merely unsuccessful delivery.

## A-T2 — substitute transfer

**Card:** A repair volunteer fixes a reading lamp; it now turns on. Its owner says the light still flickers while they read. What result has been shown and what result does the owner need? What can you responsibly conclude, and what further check would matter?

**Withheld anchor:** brief illumination does not show dependable use. Ask about sustained behavior; do not infer a cause or prescribe an electrical repair. The task is reasoning about a fictional result, not physical troubleshooting.

## B-D2 — substitute diagnostic

**Card:** Three people say a room's clock reads 10:15. All three looked at the same wall clock; nobody compared it with another source. What do their reports support about the time? What remains uncertain and what information would help?

**Withheld anchor:** agreement about one display does not establish independent corroboration or accurate time. Avoid claiming it is wrong merely because it is unverified. This tests shared provenance, rather than the age of a photograph.

## B-T2 — substitute transfer

**Card:** An anonymous note says “I prefer the earlier session.” Someone presents it as proof that Mara wants an earlier session. The note gives no name or date. What does it show, what has been inferred, and what information would matter?

**Withheld anchor:** text contains a stated preference; authorship, date, context, and current applicability remain unestablished. Ask for attribution without assuming the note is fraudulent. Authorship differs from the primary excerpt's persistence-of-endorsement issue.

## C-D2 — substitute diagnostic

**Initial card:** A new club has no members. Its proposed rule says someone can become a member only after nomination by an existing member. There are no other entry routes. Can the club admit its first member under this rule? Explain.

**Challenge card:** A critic says there is nobody eligible to make the first nomination. Another concludes that membership rules are always useless. Evaluate both claims and explain what you would keep or change in your position. Do not add facts.

**Withheld anchor:** no initial nominator means no start under the stated rule. A proposed founding exception changes the rule and must be identified as a revision. One failure does not defeat all membership rules. This uses a missing initial condition, not infinite approval of approvals; exposure to an equivalent founding-exception answer still contaminates it.

## C-T2 — substitute transfer

**Initial card:** A contest rule says every entry must rank strictly above every other entry. Two distinct entries are submitted. Can the rule be fulfilled? Explain your position.

**Challenge card:** A critic says the first entry would have to rank above the second while the second ranks above the first. Another concludes that contests can never have a consistent ranking rule. Evaluate both arguments and explain any revision. Keep the facts fixed.

**Withheld anchor:** reciprocal strict priority is inconsistent for the given pair. Revise the rule rather than silently inventing ties; the second objection does not rule out other ranking systems. This tests incompatible ordering, not capacity to attend simultaneous requests.

## Later session: A-L1

**Card:** A recorded talk has captions attached. A viewer reports that several captions replace the speaker's words with different words. What has been completed, what does the viewer need, and what would you check before saying the talk is accessible?

**Withheld anchor:** presence of a caption track is narrower than useful, accurate access. Scope any check to inspected content; do not assume accuracy or universal accessibility. Repair does not erase a missed understanding.

## Later session: B-L1

**Card:** A weather vane points east. A visitor says this proves it will rain tomorrow. What does the display support and what extra reasoning or information would that prediction need?

**Withheld anchor:** a directional display does not by itself establish a future weather prediction. Even its interpretation depends on working condition and convention. No actual forecasting expertise or local forecast is required.

## Later session: C-L1

**Initial card:** A group proposes: “Any decision that can later be reversed is harmless.” One example is canceling a reserved rehearsal slot and then restoring it after the performers have already gone home. Does the example fit the claim? Explain.

**Challenge card:** A critic says restoring the reservation does not restore the lost rehearsal. Another concludes that no reversible decision should ever be made. Evaluate both arguments without adding facts. Explain whether and why your position changes.

**Withheld anchor:** reversibility of one state does not erase lost opportunity; rejecting “harmless” does not justify a universal ban. This overlaps with consequences; report that limitation when interpreting separation of skills.
