# Script B — Humility

**B-W1 v1 · Facilitator-only script · ILLUSTRATIVE · Draft.** Approximate narration: 3 minutes plus reflection. Reveal one state at a time.

## Scene and prediction — read aloud

“A neighborhood reading group has twenty members. Five replied to a voluntary survey; all five chose Tuesday. The organizer says: ‘The group prefers Tuesday.’ Only the organizer can set the session date, and members may raise concerns. What can the organizer reasonably say from these replies? What would you want to know before choosing a date?”

Pause for a decision. Ask whether a table helps or whether individual accounts and missing voices are better represented in prose. Do not require formalization.

## Decision plate — show after the decision

**Whose preference do the replies establish? — ILLUSTRATIVE**

| Stated in the story | Proposed interpretation | Still unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Five of twenty members replied. All five chose Tuesday. | Those five selected Tuesday in this survey. | The other fifteen members' preferences and reasons for not replying. |
| The organizer may set a date. | The organizer can make a bounded decision with acknowledged uncertainty. | Whether Tuesday serves the whole group and how dissent will be heard. |

**Bounded conclusion:** Tuesday is supported by the five replies. A claim about every member goes beyond them. Choosing a date and knowing everyone's preference are different questions.

**Legend and text equivalent:** the table separates supplied facts, interpretations, and unknowns in a fictional story. It is not a measurement of actual people or a universal rule for survey adequacy. Source: B-W1 v1; objective from [strategy §2 and §8](<../../SideFX Wisdom Principles - Training Strategies.md>).

## Challenge and feedback — read after reflection

“A member objects: people who cannot use the survey might not have replied. Is that established, or a possibility to investigate?” Pause. “It is a possible explanation. The story does not tell us why fifteen people did not reply. Ask through another accessible route if feasible. Do not invent their preferences.”

Now reveal this explicitly new fictional information: “The organizer contacts six previously silent members. Four cannot attend Tuesday; two can. Which statements now change?”

“The statement that everyone prefers Tuesday is no longer tenable. The original five replies remain what they were. Nine members' views are still unknown. A date choice may remain necessary, but explain the trade-off, invite dissent, and avoid claiming complete knowledge. The organizer's permission to choose supplies no missing facts.”

## Close and production notes

Accept a bounded provisional choice, further inquiry, or postponement when justified. Do not reward blanket disbelief: the five replies support something. A simple conversation is a plausible competing solution. A falsifier of the whole-group claim is a member with a different stated preference; the new reveal supplies one. Do not teach a numerical confidence threshold.

Transition: “Say what your information supports, where you are extending it, and what could change your mind.” This lesson changes evidence; C tests arguments under fixed facts. Keep photograph and excerpt cards out of teaching. Read the plate aloud; its labels carry meaning without color.
