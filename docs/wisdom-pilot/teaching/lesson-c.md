# Script C — Adversarial survivability

**C-W1 v1 · Facilitator-only script · ILLUSTRATIVE · Draft.** Approximate narration: 3 minutes plus reflection. No fictional professor or personal confrontation is needed.

## Scene and prediction — read aloud

“A club has six members. Each meeting needs one note-taker. The elected chair may propose a rule, but the members must agree to adopt it. The chair proposes: ‘We will avoid arguments about favorites by always choosing the first name alphabetically.’ All six names are different. The chair calls this a fair way to share the work. What do you think of that claim?”

Save the decision. Then reveal: “A critic says the same member will do the work at every meeting. Another says this shows that every agreed rule is unfair. Evaluate both arguments without adding any new facts.” Pause for revision or justified retention.

Ask: “Would a diagram help, or does an account of what sharing means preserve more of the disagreement?” Do not require a model or assume equal rotation solves every value conflict.

## Decision plate — show after the decision

**What does the objection actually defeat? — ILLUSTRATIVE**

| Claim | Challenge under the same facts | Reasoned response |
|---|---|---|
| Always choosing the alphabetically first name shares the work fairly. | With fixed distinct names it repeatedly selects the same person. | It may avoid selection arguments but does not distribute the work. Clarify or revise the fairness claim. |
| Every agreed rule is unfair. | No argument about every possible rule has been supplied. | This does not follow from the failure of one rule. Consider rotation, willing volunteers, or another proposal. |

**Open decision:** members can compare proposals and their burdens before agreeing. A rotating rule distributes turns but may still need adjustments for ability, preference, or absence. Those are questions to ask, not facts supplied by this scene.

**Legend and text equivalent:** both objections concern one fictional proposal. No new observations are introduced. One objection exposes a consequence of a rule; the other makes a broader claim without support. Source: C-W1 v1; objective from [strategy §2, §3 and §8](<../../SideFX Wisdom Principles - Training Strategies.md>).

## Feedback and close

“A strong answer can change its mind, or keep an initial answer that already identified the problem. Explain which claim the objection reaches. A predictable selection method can serve one aim while failing another. Rejecting an overbroad objection does not rescue the original fairness claim.”

The fixed-name example defeats a claim that this rule shares turns. It does not establish a uniquely fair replacement. A simple roster or conversation is a credible alternative. The chair cannot unilaterally adopt a new rule under the stated facts. Keep the burden on the repeatedly selected member visible while hearing preferences from all members.

Transition: “Before defending or abandoning a claim, ask what the objection establishes and how far it reaches.” Do not preview the approval or editor cases. Give quiet reflection time; no credit for dramatic debate or agreement with the facilitator. The plate text is the complete accessible equivalent and can be used as static narration keyframes.
