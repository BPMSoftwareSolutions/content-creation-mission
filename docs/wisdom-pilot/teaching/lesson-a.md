# Script A — Consequences

**A-W1 v1 · Facilitator-only script · ILLUSTRATIVE · Draft.** Show only the current learner-facing state. Approximate narration: 3 minutes plus reflection. No circuit or motion is needed.

## Scene and prediction — read aloud

“A volunteer reserves a room for a weekend group outing. The reservation is confirmed. The chosen time overlaps the last bus home for two members, who say they cannot attend at that time. The volunteer can ask about another slot; only the group coordinator can commit to a booking change. The coordinator says: ‘The reservation succeeded, so the outing is arranged.’ What would you say should happen next, and why?”

Pause for a written decision before showing the plate's answer. Then ask: “Would a small table help explain this, or would a written account preserve something a table would lose? What should stay outside a diagram?” Treat either answer as legitimate.

## Decision plate — show after the decision

**Is the outing arranged for the people who need it? — ILLUSTRATIVE**

| What happened | What people need | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| A room reservation was confirmed. | An outing the intended participants can actually attend. | Two members report a travel conflict; other times and costs are unknown. |
| The volunteer can ask about options. | A chance for affected members to explain acceptable alternatives. | The coordinator must decide any booking change within their role. |

**Next defensible step:** ask about alternatives and hear the affected members before promising the outing is fully arranged. A booking record supports the reservation, not everyone's ability to attend.

**Legend and text equivalent:** every statement belongs to a fictional story. The table separates a completed booking, the participants' needed result, and unresolved arrangements. It does not depict execution, permission, or proof of learning. Source: A-W1 v1 in this script; editorial objective from [strategy §2, §5, §8](<../../SideFX Wisdom Principles - Training Strategies.md>).

## Challenge and feedback — read after reflection

“Another organizer says moving the outing could inconvenience people who already planned around this time. That objection matters. Asking about alternatives does not yet justify changing the booking. Compare a different time, travel support if feasible, postponement, and keeping the booking with the exclusion explicitly acknowledged. Listen to members who bear each cost. The coordinator's role does not make every choice fair.”

“What would change your judgment?” Collect an answer. Then explain: “The two members might identify an acceptable travel option, or a different time could exclude more people. That could change the next step. It would not make the original booking confirmation proof that the whole arrangement worked.”

## Close and production notes

The tempting claim is defeated by the stated travel conflict. No final time is mandated; warranted disagreement or an open decision is acceptable. A simple conversation and list of alternatives can be sufficient. Changing a booking may restore a slot but cannot necessarily recover preparation time or missed participation.

Transition: “When someone says a task is complete, ask which result they mean and who still cannot get what they need.” Reserve the library card for independent transfer; do not mention its setting or solution here.
