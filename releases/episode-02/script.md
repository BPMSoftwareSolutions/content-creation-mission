## The task finished. Did it help?

The task is finished. The confirmation arrived. But did it help? Before we talk about artificial intelligence, consider an ordinary decision: arranging an outing for a group of people.

## A confirmed reservation

A volunteer reserves a room for a weekend group outing. The booking is confirmed. But the time conflicts with the last bus home for two members. They say they cannot attend at that time.

## Who can decide?

The volunteer can ask about another slot. Only the group coordinator can commit to changing the booking. The coordinator says: The reservation succeeded, so the outing is arranged.

## Separate action, result, and uncertainty

Here is one way to separate the facts. What happened: a room reservation was confirmed. What people need: an outing the intended participants can actually attend. What remains open: two members report a travel conflict. Other times and costs are unknown.

## What the confirmation proves

The booking record supports a narrow claim: there is a reservation. It does not establish that everyone can attend. A defensible next step is to ask about alternatives and hear the affected members before promising that the outing is fully arranged.

## An objection that matters

Now another organizer objects. People have already planned around this time. Moving the outing could inconvenience them, or exclude someone else. That objection matters. Asking about alternatives does not yet justify changing the booking.

## Compare the consequences

Compare a different time, travel support if it is feasible, postponement, and keeping the booking with the exclusion explicitly acknowledged. Hear from the people who bear each cost. The coordinator has a decision role. That role does not make every choice fair.

## New facts can change the next step

Perhaps the two members identify an acceptable travel option. Perhaps a different time would exclude more people. Neither is established in this story. Either could change the next step. Neither would make the original confirmation proof that the whole arrangement worked.

## Completion is one fact

A conversation and a list of alternatives may be enough. Changing a booking does not necessarily recover preparation time or missed participation. This is the consequences lens we are starting with in SideFX: when someone says a task is complete, ask which result they mean, and who still cannot get what they need.

## Carry the question with you

Think of one completed task in your own work. Whose result might still be unresolved? Describe the missing result and the evidence you would need. Next, we will ask: What do we actually know?
